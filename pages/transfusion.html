What Is a Blood Transfusion?

A blood transfusion is a medical procedure in which donated blood or blood components are transferred into a patient's bloodstream through an intravenous (IV) line. It is a safe, common procedure that saves millions of lives every year.

Modern blood transfusions have been performed safely since the early 1900s, after Karl Landsteiner's discovery of blood groups made compatibility matching possible.

When Is a Transfusion Needed?

Surgery

Major surgeries may require blood to replace what is lost during the operation.

Trauma

Accident victims with severe blood loss need rapid transfusion to survive.

Anemia

Severe anemia patients may need red blood cells to restore oxygen-carrying capacity.

Cancer

Chemotherapy can reduce blood cell counts; transfusions help restore them.

Bleeding Disorders

Patients with hemophilia or clotting disorders may need plasma or platelets.

Childbirth

Women who experience severe bleeding during delivery may need a transfusion.

Types of Transfusions

  • Red Blood Cell Transfusion: For anemia or blood loss; restores oxygen delivery
  • Platelet Transfusion: For low platelet counts or bleeding disorders
  • Plasma Transfusion: For clotting disorders, liver disease, or severe burns
  • Whole Blood Transfusion: Rare; used mainly in trauma situations

The Transfusion Process

Step 1

Blood Typing & Crossmatching

The patient's blood type is determined, and donor blood is tested for compatibility.

Step 2

IV Line Insertion

A nurse inserts an IV line, usually in the arm, to deliver the blood.

Step 3

Transfusion Begins

Blood flows slowly at first while the patient is monitored for any reaction.

Step 4

Monitoring

Vital signs are checked regularly throughout the 1-4 hour procedure.

Step 5

Completion

The IV is removed, and the patient is observed briefly before discharge.

Safety & Risks

Blood transfusions are very safe. Donated blood undergoes rigorous testing for infectious diseases including HIV, hepatitis B and C, and syphilis. The risk of infection from a transfusion is extremely low. Mild reactions (fever, chills) can occur but are easily treated. Severe reactions are rare due to careful compatibility matching.

In the United States, the risk of HIV from a blood transfusion is approximately 1 in 1.5 million — making blood donation and transfusion one of the safest medical procedures available.
