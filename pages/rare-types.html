The extraordinary blood types that most people have never heard of

Beyond ABO and Rh

While most people know about the 8 main blood types (A+, A-, B+, B-, AB+, AB-, O+, O-), there are actually over 600 known antigens on red blood cells. The presence or absence of these antigens creates rare blood types that most people have never heard of.

A blood type is considered "rare" if it lacks antigens that 99% of people have, or has antigens that 99% of people lack. Only about 1 in 1,000 people have a rare blood type.

Rh-null

The rarest blood type in the world — fewer than 50 known people have it. These individuals lack all Rh antigens, making them universal donors for Rh-blood, but they can only receive from others with Rh-null blood.

Golden Blood (Rh-null)

Often called "golden blood," this type is so rare that only about 43 people worldwide have been identified. It is invaluable for treating patients with rare blood disorders.

AB- (AB Negative)

The rarest of the 8 main blood types, found in less than 1% of the global population. AB- plasma is the universal plasma type and can be given to patients of any blood group.

Bombay (Oh)

Named after the city where it was discovered. People with this type lack the H antigen, making them incompatible with all ABO blood except other Bombay blood. Found mainly in South Asia.

Junior (Jra-negative)

Extremely rare, found in fewer than 0.1% of the population. Pregnant women with this type may face complications, making identification critical.

Lutheran (Lub-negative)

A rare blood type that can complicate transfusions and pregnancies. Only a small number of donors are known worldwide.

Why Rare Blood Matters

For patients with rare blood types, finding compatible blood can be a matter of life and death. Blood banks maintain rare donor registries to ensure these patients can receive the blood they need. If you have a rare blood type, registering as a donor is especially important — you could be someone's only match.
