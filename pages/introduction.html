The river of life that flows through every one of us

What Is Blood?

Blood is a vital fluid that circulates through the body's cardiovascular system, delivering essential substances like oxygen and nutrients to cells while removing waste products. An average adult has about 4.5 to 5.7 liters of blood — roughly 7-8% of total body weight.

Blood is classified as a connective tissue. It consists of cells suspended in a liquid matrix called plasma, making it unique among the body's tissues.

Why Blood Matters

Blood performs several life-sustaining functions that keep us alive and healthy every single second:

  • Transportation: Carries oxygen from the lungs to tissues and carbon dioxide back; delivers nutrients from digestion; moves hormones and waste products.
  • Protection: White blood cells fight infections; antibodies and clotting factors prevent blood loss and defend against disease.
  • Regulation: Helps maintain body temperature, pH balance, and fluid volume throughout the body.

Composition of Blood

Blood is made up of four main components, each with a specific role:

Red Blood Cells (45%)

Carry oxygen via hemoglobin. About 5 million per microliter of blood.

White Blood Cells (<1%)

The immune system's soldiers. Fight bacteria, viruses, and infections.

Platelets (<1%)

Cell fragments that form clots to stop bleeding and heal wounds.

Plasma (55%)

The liquid portion — water, proteins, salts, and dissolved substances.

A Brief History

The understanding of blood has evolved dramatically over centuries. In 1628, William Harvey discovered that blood circulates through the body. In 1901, Karl Landsteiner identified the main blood groups (A, B, AB, O), earning him the Nobel Prize and making safe transfusions possible. Since then, blood science has saved countless lives.

The discovery of blood groups by Karl Landsteiner is considered one of the most important medical breakthroughs in history — it made blood transfusions safe for the first time.
