The golden liquid that carries everything through your veins

What Is Plasma?

Plasma is the pale yellow liquid portion of blood that holds the blood cells in suspension. It makes up about 55% of your total blood volume. Plasma is about 92% water, with the remaining 8% consisting of proteins, salts, lipids, and glucose.

One plasma donation can be processed into multiple life-saving therapies, treating burn victims, trauma patients, and people with clotting disorders.

What Plasma Contains

  • Water (92%): The primary component that carries everything else
  • Proteins (7%): Albumin, globulins, and clotting factors like fibrinogen
  • Electrolytes: Sodium, potassium, calcium, and chloride ions
  • Nutrients: Glucose, amino acids, and fatty acids
  • Hormones: Insulin, thyroxine, and cortisol
  • Waste Products: Urea and creatinine being transported to kidneys

Functions of Plasma

Transport

Carries nutrients, hormones, and proteins to cells and waste products to organs for removal.

Clotting

Contains clotting factors like fibrinogen that stop bleeding when you are injured.

Immunity

Carries antibodies (immunoglobulins) that fight infections and disease.

Pressure

Albumin maintains blood volume and osmotic pressure, preventing fluid loss.

Plasma Donation

Plasma donation differs from whole blood donation. During plasmapheresis, blood is drawn, the plasma is separated out, and the red blood cells are returned to the donor. This means you can donate plasma more frequently — up to every 28 days — because your body replenishes plasma quickly.

AB plasma is the "universal plasma donor" — it can be given to patients of any blood type. AB plasma donors are especially valuable.

Who Needs Plasma?

  • Burn victims who have lost significant fluid
  • Trauma patients with severe bleeding
  • Patients with liver disease who cannot produce clotting factors
  • People with immune deficiencies who need antibody replacement
  • Patients with clotting disorders like hemophilia
